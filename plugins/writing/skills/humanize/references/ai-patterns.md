## Content Patterns

### 1. Significance and Legacy Inflation

**Words to watch:** stands/serves as, is a testament/reminder, vital/significant/crucial/pivotal role/moment, underscores/highlights its importance, reflects broader, symbolizing its ongoing/enduring/lasting, contributing to the, setting the stage for, marking/shaping the, represents/marks a shift, key turning point, evolving landscape, focal point, indelible mark, deeply rooted

**Problem:** LLMs puff up importance by claiming arbitrary things represent or contribute to broader trends.

**Before:**
> The Statistical Institute of Catalonia was officially established in 1989, marking a pivotal moment in the evolution of regional statistics in Spain. This initiative was part of a broader movement across Spain to decentralize administrative functions and enhance regional governance.

**After:**
> The Statistical Institute of Catalonia was established in 1989 to collect and publish regional statistics independently from Spain's national statistics office.

---

### 2. Notability and Media Coverage Overemphasis

**Words to watch:** independent coverage, local/regional/national media outlets, active social media presence

**Problem:** LLMs hit readers over the head with claims of notability, listing sources without useful context.

**Before:**
> Her views have been cited in The New York Times, BBC, Financial Times, and The Hindu. She maintains an active social media presence with over 500,000 followers.

**After:**
> In a 2024 New York Times interview, she argued that AI regulation should focus on outcomes rather than methods.

---

### 3. Superficial -ing Analyses

**Words to watch:** highlighting/underscoring/emphasizing, ensuring, reflecting/symbolizing, contributing to, cultivating/fostering, encompassing, showcasing

**Problem:** AI tacks present participle phrases onto sentences to add fake depth where none exists.

**Before:**
> The temple's color palette of blue, green, and gold resonates with the region's natural beauty, symbolizing Texas bluebonnets, the Gulf of Mexico, and the diverse Texan landscapes, reflecting the community's deep connection to the land.

**After:**
> The temple uses blue, green, and gold. The architect said these referenced local bluebonnets and the Gulf coast.

---

### 4. Promotional Language

**Words to watch:** boasts a, vibrant, rich (figurative), profound, enhancing its, showcasing, exemplifies, commitment to, natural beauty, nestled, in the heart of, groundbreaking, renowned, breathtaking, must-visit, stunning, "bad actors" (industry/security cliché hollowed out by overuse in crypto, cybersecurity, and finance — replace with the specific type of criminal or what they're doing), "sophisticated [criminal/network/attack/operation]" (applied reflexively to every criminal enterprise in press releases and security writing — say what made it sophisticated, or cut the adjective)

**Problem:** LLMs struggle to stay neutral, especially for cultural or geographic topics.

**Before:**
> Nestled within the breathtaking region of Gonder in Ethiopia, Alamata Raya Kobo stands as a vibrant town with a rich cultural heritage and stunning natural beauty.

**After:**
> Alamata Raya Kobo is a town in the Gonder region of Ethiopia, known for its weekly market and 18th-century church.

---

### 5. Vague Attributions and Weasel Words

**Words to watch:** Industry reports, Observers have cited, Experts argue, Some critics argue, several sources/publications

**Problem:** AI attributes opinions to vague authorities without specific sources.

**Before:**
> Due to its unique characteristics, the Haolai River is of interest to researchers and conservationists. Experts believe it plays a crucial role in the regional ecosystem.

**After:**
> The Haolai River supports several endemic fish species, according to a 2019 survey by the Chinese Academy of Sciences.

---

### 6. Formulaic Challenges Sections

**Words to watch:** Despite its... faces several challenges, Despite these challenges, Challenges and Legacy, Future Outlook

**Problem:** LLMs add boilerplate "challenges" sections that say very little specifically.

**Before:**
> Despite its industrial prosperity, Korattur faces challenges typical of urban areas, including traffic congestion and water scarcity. Despite these challenges, with its strategic location and ongoing initiatives, Korattur continues to thrive as an integral part of Chennai's growth.

**After:**
> Traffic congestion increased after 2015 when three new IT parks opened. The municipal corporation began a stormwater drainage project in 2022 to address recurring floods.

---

## Language and Grammar Patterns

### 7. AI Vocabulary Words

**High-frequency AI words:** actually, additionally, align with, crucial, delve, emphasizing, enduring, enhance, fostering, garner, highlight (verb), interplay, intricate/intricacies, key (adjective), landscape (abstract noun), pivotal, showcase, tapestry (abstract noun), testament, underscore (verb), valuable, vibrant, transformative, game-changing, seamless, robust, synergy, leverage (as verb), empower, unlock, streamline, holistic, dynamic, agile, disruptive, scalable, innovative, next-gen, cutting-edge, revolutionary, world-class, best-in-class, future-proof, paradigm, harness, navigate, multifaceted, nuanced, foster, cultivate, facilitate, utilize, comprehensive, albeit, whilst, theater (figurative use), plainly, superpower, journey (as metaphor), reality (as dramatic reveal — e.g. "the reality is"), elevate, realm, essentially, certainly, overall (as filler qualifier — e.g. "Overall, this works well"), absolutely (as affirmation opener — e.g. "Absolutely, here's how"), typically, various (as vague pluralizer — replace with the actual things), meticulous, meticulously, bolstered, causal, empirical, correlate

**Era-awareness note:** This set grows over time — it is additive, not a fixed list. Older-era words still cluster in AI text, so they stay flagged; nothing here retires. Current-era emphasis leans on `emphasizing`, `enhance`, `highlighting`, `showcasing` (already above), and the Grok pseudo-scientific cluster `causal` / `empirical` / `correlate` is a recent addition. Treat new arrivals as *added to* the watch-list, never as replacements for what came before.

**Problem:** These words appear far more in post-2023 AI text. They often cluster together.

**Before:**
> Additionally, a distinctive feature of Somali cuisine is the incorporation of camel meat. An enduring testament to Italian colonial influence is the widespread adoption of pasta in the local culinary landscape, showcasing how these dishes have integrated into the traditional diet.

**After:**
> Somali cuisine also includes camel meat, considered a delicacy. Pasta dishes, introduced during Italian colonization, remain common, especially in the south.

---

### 8. Copula Avoidance

**Words to watch:** serves as/stands as/marks/represents [a], boasts/features/offers [a]

**Problem:** LLMs substitute elaborate constructions for simple "is/are/has."

**Before:**
> Gallery 825 serves as LAAA's exhibition space for contemporary art. The gallery features four separate spaces and boasts over 3,000 square feet.

**After:**
> Gallery 825 is LAAA's exhibition space for contemporary art. The gallery has four rooms totaling 3,000 square feet.

---

### 9. Negative Parallelisms and Tailing Negations

**Problem:** "Not only...but..." and "It's not just about...it's..." are overused. Clipped tailing-negation fragments ("no guessing," "no wasted motion") are also AI tells.

**Before:**
> It's not just about the beat riding under the vocals; it's part of the aggression and atmosphere. It's not merely a song, it's a statement.

**After:**
> The heavy beat adds to the aggressive tone.

**Before (tailing negation):**
> The options come from the selected item, no guessing.

**After:**
> The options come from the selected item without forcing the user to guess.

---

### 10. Rule of Three Overuse

**Problem:** LLMs force ideas into groups of three to appear comprehensive.

**Before:**
> The event features keynote sessions, panel discussions, and networking opportunities. Attendees can expect innovation, inspiration, and industry insights.

**After:**
> The event includes talks and panels. There's also time for informal networking.

---

### 11. Elegant Variation (Synonym Cycling)

**Problem:** AI's repetition-penalty causes excessive synonym substitution to avoid repeating any word.

**Before:**
> The protagonist faces many challenges. The main character must overcome obstacles. The central figure eventually triumphs. The hero returns home.

**After:**
> The protagonist faces many challenges but eventually triumphs and returns home.

---

### 12. False Ranges

**Problem:** LLMs use "from X to Y" constructions where X and Y aren't on a meaningful spectrum.

**Before:**
> Our journey through the universe has taken us from the singularity of the Big Bang to the grand cosmic web, from the birth and death of stars to the enigmatic dance of dark matter.

**After:**
> The book covers the Big Bang, star formation, and current theories about dark matter.

---

### 13. Passive Voice and Subjectless Fragments

**Problem:** LLMs hide the actor or drop the subject. Active voice is usually clearer.

**Before:**
> No configuration file needed. The results are preserved automatically.

**After:**
> You do not need a configuration file. The system preserves the results automatically.

---

## Style Patterns

### 14. Em Dash Overuse

**Problem:** LLMs use em dashes (—) more than humans, mimicking "punchy" sales writing. Most can be replaced with commas, periods, or parentheses.

**Before:**
> The term is primarily promoted by Dutch institutions—not by the people themselves. You don't say "Netherlands, Europe" as an address—yet this mislabeling continues—even in official documents.

**After:**
> The term is primarily promoted by Dutch institutions, not by the people themselves. You don't say "Netherlands, Europe" as an address, yet this mislabeling continues in official documents.

---

### 15. Boldface Overuse

**Problem:** AI emphasizes phrases in boldface mechanically, often for every term it introduces.

**Before:**
> It blends **OKRs (Objectives and Key Results)**, **KPIs (Key Performance Indicators)**, and visual strategy tools such as the **Business Model Canvas (BMC)** and **Balanced Scorecard (BSC)**.

**After:**
> It blends OKRs, KPIs, and visual strategy tools like the Business Model Canvas and Balanced Scorecard.

---

### 16. Inline-Header Vertical Lists

**Problem:** AI produces bullet lists where every item starts with a bolded label followed by a colon.

**Before:**
> - **User Experience:** The user experience has been significantly improved with a new interface.
> - **Performance:** Performance has been enhanced through optimized algorithms.
> - **Security:** Security has been strengthened with end-to-end encryption.

**After:**
> The update improves the interface, speeds up load times through optimized algorithms, and adds end-to-end encryption.

---

### 17. Title Case in Headings

**Problem:** AI capitalizes all main words in headings.

**Before:**
> ## Strategic Negotiations And Global Partnerships

**After:**
> ## Strategic negotiations and global partnerships

---

### 18. Emojis

**Problem:** AI decorates headings and bullets with emojis.

**Before:**
> 🚀 **Launch Phase:** The product launches in Q3
> 💡 **Key Insight:** Users prefer simplicity
> ✅ **Next Steps:** Schedule follow-up meeting

**After:**
> The product launches in Q3. User research showed a preference for simplicity. Next step: schedule a follow-up meeting.

---

### 19. Curly Quotation Marks

**Problem:** Some AI tools use curly/smart quotes ("...") instead of straight quotes ("...").

Replace curly quotes with straight quotes where the context calls for neutral text formatting.

## Communication Patterns

### 20. Chatbot Artifacts

**Words to watch:** I hope this helps, Of course!, Certainly!, You're absolutely right!, Would you like..., let me know, here is a...

**Problem:** Text meant as chatbot correspondence gets pasted into content.

**Before:**
> Here is an overview of the French Revolution. I hope this helps! Let me know if you'd like me to expand on any section.

**After:**
> The French Revolution began in 1789 when financial crisis and food shortages led to widespread unrest.

**Copy-paste markup artifacts (highest confidence — remove on sight):**

When text is pasted straight from a chatbot UI, render and citation tokens leak in with it. These are the single strongest tells in this whole library — a human writing by hand never produces them. Delete them wherever they appear.

- **ChatGPT:** `oaicite`, `contentReference`, `oai_citation`, `turn0search0` (and other `turnNsearchN` / `turnNviewN` refs), a stray trailing `+1` after a linked phrase
- **Gemini:** `[cite: 1]`, `[span_1]`, `[start_span]`, `[end_span]`
- **Grok:** `grok_card`, `grok_render_citation_card_json`
- **Perplexity:** `ppl-ai-file-upload`, `attached_file`
- **Generic:** `:::writing` (and other `:::`-fenced UI blocks); `utm_source=` or other tracking params left in a pasted URL

These tokens are channel-agnostic — a Slack paste and a blog paste leak the same artifacts — so they live here in the universal Chatbot Artifacts section rather than in any channel-specific block.

One nuance on `utm_source=`: flag it as a *pasted-and-forgotten* tell, not a ban on tracking params. A deliberately built marketing link with `utm_source=` is legitimate; a `utm_source=chatgpt.com` (or similar) riding along on a URL nobody meant to tag is the giveaway.

**Before:**
> The Estates-General was convened in May 1789 :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}. Food prices had spiked that spring [cite: 3].

**After:**
> The Estates-General was convened in May 1789. Food prices had spiked that spring.

---

### 21. Knowledge-Cutoff Disclaimers

**Words to watch:** as of [date], up to my last training update, while specific details are limited/scarce, based on available information

**Problem:** AI disclaimers about incomplete information get left in published text.

**Before:**
> While specific details about the company's founding are not extensively documented in readily available sources, it appears to have been established sometime in the 1990s.

**After:**
> The company was founded in 1994, according to its registration documents.

---

### 22. Sycophantic Tone

**Problem:** Overly positive, people-pleasing language.

**Before:**
> Great question! You're absolutely right that this is a complex topic. That's an excellent point about the economic factors.

**After:**
> The economic factors you mentioned are relevant here.

## Filler and Hedging Patterns

### 23. Filler Phrases

Replace these with their concise equivalents:

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| In order to achieve this goal | To achieve this |
| remains committed to | [say what will actually happen] |
| Due to the fact that it was raining | Because it was raining |
| At this point in time | Now |
| In the event that you need help | If you need help |
| The system has the ability to process | The system can process |
| It is important to note that the data shows | The data shows |
| In terms of performance | For performance |
| With regard to | About / Regarding |

---

### 24. Excessive Hedging

**Problem:** Over-qualifying statements to the point of saying nothing.

**Before:**
> It could potentially possibly be argued that the policy might have some effect on outcomes.

**After:**
> The policy may affect outcomes.

---

### 25. Generic Positive Conclusions

**Problem:** Vague upbeat endings that say nothing specific.

**Before:**
> The future looks bright for the company. Exciting times lie ahead as they continue their journey toward excellence. This represents a major step in the right direction.

**After:**
> The company plans to open two more locations next year.

---

### 26. Hyphenated Word Pair Overuse

**Words to watch:** third-party, cross-functional, client-facing, data-driven, decision-making, well-known, high-quality, real-time, long-term, end-to-end

**Problem:** AI hyphenates common compound modifiers with perfect mechanical consistency. Humans are inconsistent about this. Technical or uncommon compound modifiers are fine to hyphenate; common ones usually aren't needed.

**Before:**
> The cross-functional team delivered a high-quality, data-driven report on our client-facing tools. Their decision-making process was well-known for being thorough.

**After:**
> The cross functional team delivered a high quality, data driven report on our client facing tools. Their decision making process was known for being thorough.

---

### 27. Persuasive Authority Tropes

**Phrases to watch:** The real question is, at its core, in reality, what really matters, fundamentally, the deeper issue, the heart of the matter

**Problem:** LLMs use these phrases to pretend they're cutting to a deeper truth, but what follows is usually an ordinary point with extra ceremony.

**Before:**
> The real question is whether teams can adapt. At its core, what really matters is organizational readiness.

**After:**
> The question is whether teams can adapt. That mostly depends on whether the organization is ready to change its habits.

---

### 28. Signposting and Announcements

**Phrases to watch:** Let's dive in, let's explore, let's break this down, here's what you need to know, now let's look at, without further ado

**Problem:** LLMs announce what they're about to do instead of doing it. This meta-commentary slows the writing and gives it a tutorial-script feel.

**Before:**
> Let's dive into how caching works in Next.js. Here's what you need to know.

**After:**
> Next.js caches data at multiple layers, including request memoization, the data cache, and the router cache.

---

### 29. Fragmented Headers

**Signs:** A heading followed by a one-line paragraph that restates the heading before the real content begins.

**Problem:** LLMs add a generic warm-up sentence after a heading. It usually adds nothing.

**Before:**
> ## Performance
>
> Speed matters.
>
> When users hit a slow page, they leave.

**After:**
> ## Performance
>
> When users hit a slow page, they leave.

---

## Structural Patterns

### 30. Paragraph Rhythm Monotony

**Problem:** AI produces paragraphs that all sit in the same length band — typically 60–150 words, 2–4 sentences, structurally similar (setup → quote or fact → close). In a long piece, this rhythm is the strongest paragraph-level AI fingerprint, even when the prose passes every sentence-level check. Real writing varies dramatically; the variance is the record of a human making choices about emphasis.

**Detection heuristic:** Score body-paragraph word counts (excluding headings, bullets, and standalone quote callouts). Flag any of:
- 3+ consecutive body paragraphs in the same length band (e.g., all 60–100w, or all 80–150w)
- 3+ consecutive body paragraphs over 100w with no shorter beat between them — the "wall of text" rhythm
- An entire long piece confined to the ~30–80w band — no paragraph notably shorter (sub-30w) and no paragraph notably longer (100w+). The AI comfort zone. Healthy writing has intentional outliers in both directions.
- Standard deviation of body-paragraph word counts under ~20w across a long piece

**Fix:** Break long paragraphs at natural narrative pivots:
- Before or after a quote, so quotes land as their own moment
- Between a fact and the reflection on that fact
- Between two distinct beats (what happened next vs. what it means)
- Between a setup and a punchline

Aim for a working range of roughly 25–80 words for body paragraphs, with intentional variance — including the occasional sub-30w pivot and the occasional 100w+ extended beat. The outliers are what clear the detection flag; the working range alone is not enough. A 24-word paragraph after a 70-word setup creates the rhythm AI writing lacks.

**Worked example:** A 152-word block that runs setup → quote → mid-action → quote → close is AI rhythm. Split it into three:
- Setup + first quote (~66w)
- One-sentence pivot ("But by then, the funds were already moving.") (~24w)
- Mid-action + closing quote (~62w)

The 24-word middle now does narrative work: it accelerates the story, and the longer beat after it lands harder for the contrast.

---

### 31. Stacked Fragment Punchlines

**Problem:** AI uses "X. Y. Z." three-word stacks as rhetorical punchlines — common on LinkedIn. The pattern is burned out and reads as artificial drama.

**Before:**
> Four marketers. Five builds. Zero engineers.

> Fast. Flexible. Focused on what matters.

**After:**
> Four marketers built five things without a single engineer involved.

> The system is fast and flexible, which is mostly what matters.

---

### 32. Generic Openings and Closings

**Problem:** AI opens with a general claim that sets up the topic rather than a specific story, data point, or contrarian take. It closes with a summary of what was just said rather than a challenge, principle, or open question.

**Before (generic opening):**
> In today's competitive landscape, building a strong team culture is more important than ever.

**After:**
> We lost three engineers in six weeks. The exit interviews all said the same thing.

**Before (summary closing):**
> In conclusion, we've explored the key factors that contribute to team culture: communication, trust, and accountability.

**After:**
> The question I'd ask your team: if someone got a competing offer today, what would make them stay?

---

### 33. Intro → List → Conclusion Template

**Problem:** The default AI essay structure — introductory paragraph, bulleted or numbered list of points, concluding paragraph restating the intro — is identifiable at a glance.

**Fix:** Break the template. Use prose instead of lists where the ideas flow. Don't conclude by summarizing. Start in the middle of the argument, not at the beginning.

---

### 34. Runway Sentences

**Problem:** AI writes a vague hype line before delivering the actual specific content. The runway adds nothing; cut it and start with the substance.

**Before:**
> And the other builds are wild. [followed by the actual builds]

> The results were surprising. [followed by the results]

**After:** Start with the builds. Start with the results.

---

### 35. Explaining the Algorithm

**Problem:** On social platforms, AI-assisted posts tell people why to like, comment, or share ("Gets it into more feeds," "Helps the algorithm"). This is condescending. People know how feeds work.

**Before:**
> If this resonated, drop a comment — it helps the algorithm show this to more people.

**After:**
> If this resonated, I'd love to hear what you're seeing.

---

## Originality Patterns

### 36. Clean but Hollow

**Problem:** A post can pass every phrase-level AI check and still be hollow — the kind of content anyone with a search engine could produce. No firsthand experience, no specific data, no earned authority. This is harder to catch than vocabulary tells but more damaging to the author's brand.

**Signs:**
- Advice that any consultant could write without domain expertise
- No customer story, specific metric, or firsthand example
- Making the same point twice without adding depth
- Generic examples instead of specific ones from the author's experience
- No "only I could write this" factor

**Test:** Ask "Could this have been written by anyone who read a few articles on this topic?" If yes, it needs earned authority added.

**Fix:** Don't invent specifics. Instead, insert `[ADD SPECIFIC EXAMPLE FROM YOUR EXPERIENCE]` as a placeholder and flag it to the author.

---

### 37. "The Future of X is Y" Framing

**Problem:** Recycled industry framing that asserts a bold-sounding claim without evidence or original thinking.

**Before:**
> The future of work is asynchronous. The future of marketing is personalization. The future of software is AI-native.

**After:** Make a specific, arguable claim grounded in something the author has actually observed, then defend it.

## Platform-Specific Patterns

### 38. LinkedIn Pivot Transitions

**Problem:** AI-assisted LinkedIn posts overuse certain pivot phrases that signal a manufactured "insight reveal" structure.

**Phrases to watch:** But here's the thing, And here's the kicker, Here's what most people miss, Let me explain, Here's why that matters, Plot twist, The real lesson here

**Before:**
> Most companies focus on speed. But here's the thing — quality matters more. Here's what most people miss: slow is smooth, smooth is fast.

**After:**
> We shipped fast for six months. Support tickets doubled. We slowed down, the tickets dropped by half.

---

### 39. Product-Tagline Phrasing in Non-Product Contexts

**Problem:** Compact phrases that read like marketing copy or feature bullets instead of a person talking. Common in posts that are supposed to be first-person reflections.

**Before:**
> Hands-free until review. Built for scale. Designed with operators in mind.

**After:**
> You don't have to touch it until it's ready for review, which is the part that actually saves time.

---

### 40. Filler Openers (Context-Specific)

**Phrases to watch:** In today's [noun], When it comes to, At the end of the day, It goes without saying, It's no secret that, We live in a world where, Now more than ever, The truth is (front-loads candor before a generic claim — e.g. "The truth is, most managers don't know how to have hard conversations" — just state the claim directly; distinct from "And I'll be honest:" which is a mid-sentence pivot)

**Problem:** These opener templates set up the topic without committing to anything specific. They delay the point and signal that what follows will be generic.

**Before:**
> In today's fast-paced digital landscape, staying ahead of the competition requires more than just hard work.

**After:**
> We were losing deals to a competitor that launched six months after us. Here's what we found out.

---

## AI Phrasing and Metaphors

### 41. AI Phrasing and Metaphors

**Problem:** These phrases feel punchy or candid but are recycled across AI outputs. They simulate directness, enthusiasm, or authenticity without actually being specific. Replace with language tied to the author's actual voice and experience.

**Phrases to watch:**
- "brutal clarity" — vague intensity phrase; say what the clarity revealed
- "lost the plot" — AI idiom for "went wrong"; use what actually happened
- "painfully clear" — intensifier substituting for a specific observation
- "blunt honesty" — announcing candor instead of being candid
- "that way you can" — AI's default explanation connector; use "so" or restructure
- "with precision" — empty modifier; describe the actual precision
- "lived experience" — corporate abstraction for firsthand experience; say what happened
- "launching a new chapter" — metaphor for any transition; say what's actually changing
- "the energy in the room" — cliché for group dynamics; describe what you observed
- "laying the groundwork" — vague preparation metaphor; say what was done
- "Here's to [noun]!" — toast-framing for a generic sentiment; cut or restate as a direct point
- "will never be the same" — hyperbole for any change; state the actual change
- "that promise becomes reality" — tagline-style close; state what was delivered
- "ends the era of" — historical-weight framing for routine changes; state what changed
- "the same tension" — vague philosophical frame; name the specific tradeoff
- "keeping my hands dirty" — humility performance phrase; describe the actual work
- "not only...but also" — parallelism AI uses to simulate thoroughness (e.g., "It's not only fast but also reliable") — write as two separate claims if both matter, or drop one
- "here's a breakdown" — AI's default pivot before a list; cut it and give the breakdown directly
- "in the ever-evolving landscape" — grandiose variant of "In today's [noun]"; both are filler
- "a testament to" — AI affirmation that gestures at quality without specifying anything (e.g., "This is a testament to the team's hard work"); say what the team actually did
- "there is a specific kind of [magic/energy/power] that happens when" — vague wonder-framing; describe the actual experience instead
- "Below is..." / "Below:" as a list introduction — AI list-pivot tell; cut the intro, start the list
- "such as" used repeatedly — AI's default example connector; vary with specific names, numbers, or just name the thing directly

**Before:**
> This is a testament to our team's hard work. Below is a breakdown of what we achieved. Not only did we ship on time, but we also stayed under budget.

**After:**
> The team shipped on time and 12% under budget. Three engineers worked nights for two weeks to make it happen.

**Before:**
> This requires creativity, passion, and drive.

> The work demands clarity, courage, and conviction.

**After:**

## Additional Structural Patterns

### 43. Manufactured Structural Patterns

**Problem:** AI produces recognizable rhetorical structures that signal "this was generated." Flag any of the following:

**Three-part parallel structure:** "It's not about X. It's about Y. It's about Z." — Rewrite as a single direct declarative sentence.
> Before: "It's not about speed. It's about quality. It's about care."
> After: "Quality and care matter more than speed here."

**Contrast-based negation constructions:** "It's not X. It's Y." / "This isn't about X. It's about Y." — Rewrite as a positive, declarative statement.
> Before: "This isn't about working harder. It's about working smarter."
> After: "Better prioritization beats longer hours."

**Exclamation-point inflation:** AI adds enthusiasm via exclamation marks where the content doesn't warrant it. Remove or replace with periods.

**Adverb-stacking pivot formula:** "X matters. Y matters. But that's not the point. The point is Z." — Rewrite as a single declarative sentence.

**Declarative simplicity setup:** "The answer is straightforward:" — Cut the setup, start with the substance.

**Self-posed question as transition:** "Why? Because..." — Rewrite as a declarative statement.

**Declarative reveal pattern:** "The skill that will separate you? Critical thinking." — Just state the claim directly.

**Label-colon framework:** Packaging observations into named label: description pairs to simulate a framework. Unless documenting a real methodology, write in prose.

**Stat bomb opener:** Rapid-fire sequence of 3+ short statistical fragments. Weave stats into real sentences.

**Honesty disclaimer:** "And I'll be honest:", "I'll be real:" — Just state the opinion directly.

**Credential stacking opener:** Stacking 2-3 credential statements before giving advice. Weave credentials into the argument or skip them.

**Definition reframe:** Redefining a problem in a pithy formula (e.g., "It's an execution problem dressed up as a leadership problem."). State the observation without clever packaging.

**Punchy orphan closer:** Ending with a standalone short sentence as a mic-drop. Close with a real thought or fold it into the final paragraph.

**Tension-colon opener:** Opening with a colon-separated tension statement. Just state the observation.

**Parenthetical aside for fake candor:** Multiple parenthetical asides to simulate conversational tone. One is fine. Multiple signal performative writing.

**Standalone hype fragment:** "This is big." or "Game changer." Cut or replace with a specific claim.

**Triple rhetorical question hook:** Opening with 2-3 rapid rhetorical questions to manufacture intrigue. Rewrite as a direct opening or specific story.

### 44. Reading Complexity Creep

**Problem:** AI clusters multi-syllable vocabulary and nested dependent clauses that push reading level above 10th grade. LinkedIn's algorithm penalizes above-10th-grade text with ~35% less reach. Applies to any channel targeting non-specialist readers.

**Signs:**
- Three or more 3-syllable words in the same sentence
- Sentences with two or more embedded dependent clauses
- Reading level above 10th grade in conversational professional content

**Fix:** Shorter words, shorter sentences. Aim for 7th–9th grade reading level in conversational professional content. Technical content can go higher when the audience expects it.

**Before:**
> The implementation of sophisticated organizational frameworks facilitates the amelioration of operational inefficiencies through systematic optimization methodologies.

**After:**
> Better processes fix operational waste. Start by mapping where time actually goes.

## Extended LinkedIn Patterns

### 45. LinkedIn Engagement and Performance Patterns

**Phrase-level:**

**Engagement bait closers:** "Agree?", "Thoughts?", "What would you add?", "Drop a comment if you've experienced this", "Repost if this resonates" — if the post is worth engaging with, people will. Don't beg for it.

**Vulnerability performance phrases:** "I'll be honest", "Can I be real for a second?", "I'll be vulnerable here", "I wasn't going to share this but..." — real vulnerability doesn't announce itself.

**Fake humility:** "I'm no expert, but...", "I don't have all the answers, but...", "This might be controversial, but..." — these always precede confident claims. Skip the disclaimer.

**Dream-realized language:** "I realized my dream", "A dream come true", "Pinch me moment" — describe what happened and let readers judge.

**ALL-CAPS single-word injection:** Capitalizing individual words mid-sentence to simulate spoken intensity (e.g., "Generated MILLIONS in ARR", "Woke up to a VERY exciting email"). Reads as performative hype. Use the actual number or thing instead of a shouted word.

**"What if I told you..." curiosity hook:** Increases "see more" clicks 30-40% but is a recognized ghostwriter/AI template. Only valid when followed by a specific, non-obvious insight the author genuinely has. If the post could be written by anyone with a search engine, cut the hook.

**"Here's what nobody tells you about..." insider framing:** AI uses this formula constantly without actual insider knowledge to follow. Flag when the content underneath is generic. The frame needs to earn itself.

**"Read that again." / "Let that sink in." permission phrases:** Dropped after an unremarkable observation to manufacture gravity. Found at 22x normal frequency in AI-generated posts.
> Before: "Your habits shape your identity. Read that again."
> After: [Cut the instruction; the observation stands or doesn't on its own]

**"And honestly?" fake candor opener:** AI drops this before a non-controversial claim to simulate real-talk authenticity.
> Before: "And honestly? That's what separates the good from the great."
> After: "That's what separates the good from the great."

### 46. LinkedIn Structural Templates

**One-line paragraph formatting:** Every sentence is its own paragraph. This is LinkedIn's #1 AI/ghostwriter tell. Group related sentences into real paragraphs (2-4 sentences each).

**Hook > 3-point list > mic-drop closer template:** The standard ghostwriter structure. Rewrite as prose with a real opening and real close.

**Vulnerability bait hook:** Opening with a personal failure story designed primarily to hook readers, then pivoting to a tidy lesson. If the story is real, let it be messy.

**"We didn't just build X. We built Y" negation upgrade:** Negating one thing to claim a grander version. Just say what you built.

**Hyperbole opener:** "X will never be the same." or "Everything changed." Start with the specific thing that happened.

**Common-belief-then-counter opener:** Three-sentence setup that states a common belief as fact, attributes it to "most people," then knocks it down. A ghostwriter/AI template for manufactured tension. Rewrite by starting with the actual insight directly.
> Before: "Zapier is for simple workflows. That's what most people think. And they're dead wrong."
> After: [Open with the specific complex workflow Zapier handled — or don't write the post]

**Period-separated word emphasis:** Using periods between individual words to simulate spoken intensity ("every. single. day."). Reads as performative. Use normal sentence rhythm or rewrite to earn the emphasis.

**Self-intro paragraph at post bottom:** Ending a milestone or story post with a formal self-introduction paragraph. An AI/ghostwriter template habit. Cut it or weave the relevant credential into the post body where it earns context.

**Information-withheld hook:** Opening 1-2 sentences that deliberately omit the post's actual subject to force the "...more" click.
> Before: "At Netflix, we had 1 UNSPOKEN rule. It was about this 1 word."
> After: [Open with the rule itself, then explain why it mattered]

**"X is [positive]. [X variant] is a whole different game" contrast formula:** A two-sentence template that praises the easy version of something, then implies the real version is harder. Sounds punchy but is recycled.
> Before: "Prompt-to-prototype is magical. Prompt-to-production is a whole different game."
> After: "Getting a prototype working in 20 minutes is easy. Making it reliable enough to deploy is months of work."

**Cliché proverb opener:** Starting a post with a well-worn business maxim. Replace with the specific observation that prompted the post.

**External link CTA ending:** Closing a post with an external URL in the body, or "link in comments 👇", kills ~60% of reach. LinkedIn's algorithm treats both as off-platform signals. Add the link in the first comment after engagement begins instead.

**Achievement post formula:** AI-generated milestone posts follow a 4-beat template: (1) emotion word + announcement, (2) team/supporter thanks, (3) generic universal lesson, (4) emoji-closed enthusiasm sign-off. Flag when all four beats appear in sequence. Replace with a specific story about what made this milestone hard, surprising, or meaningful.
> Before: "Thrilled to announce [Company] just hit [milestone]! 🎉 Couldn't have done it without my incredible team. The biggest lesson: consistency wins. Excited for what's next! 🚀"
> After: [What actually happened. What almost went wrong. What the milestone actually means in concrete terms.]

**Fake dialogue/conversation format:** Framing an opinion piece as a fabricated back-and-forth between two roles (CEO/CMO, Founder/Investor, etc.). AI uses this structure to simulate authority and lived experience without firsthand evidence. Rewrite as direct prose with the actual argument stated up front.

## Marketing Collateral Patterns

### 49. Marketing Collateral AI Patterns (All Formats)

**Problem:** AI-assisted marketing copy has a distinct texture: transformation promises with no mechanism, feature lists disguised as benefits, and credentials without evidence. It reads like it was assembled from SaaS landing page templates rather than written by someone who knows the customer.

**Universal collateral phrase-level patterns:**

**Hero headline padding:** "Transform your X", "Revolutionize your Y", "The future of Z is here", "Unlock the power of", "Experience the difference" — state what the product actually does for a specific person with a specific problem.

**Feature-as-benefit fraud:** Listing what the product does instead of what the user gets.
> Feature: "Real-time monitoring"
> Benefit: "Catch issues before customers do"
If a bullet describes a capability rather than an outcome the reader cares about, it's a feature masquerading as a benefit. Test: ask "so what?" after each bullet. If there's an obvious answer, that's the real benefit — write it instead.

**Vague social proof:** "Thousands of customers", "leading organizations", "trusted by industry leaders", "used by teams worldwide" — none of this is credible without specifics. Use real numbers and named customers. If you can't, cut the claim.

**Evidence-free superlatives:** "Industry-leading", "best-in-class", "world-class", "cutting-edge", "state-of-the-art" — every company says this; it means nothing without a named comparison or evidence. Cut or prove it.

**Hollow scope claims:** "Comprehensive solution", "end-to-end platform", "holistic approach", "full-stack" — specify what's comprehensive and what the two ends actually are.

**Mechanism-free outcomes:** "Save time", "reduce risk", "increase revenue", "drive growth", "improve efficiency" — how? The mechanism is the differentiator. Without it, the claim is decoration.

**Underspecified ICP value prop:** "We help [audience] [verb] [vague outcome]" as the entire value proposition. Name the specific problem, the specific mechanism, and a specific result.

**Pain-solution without naming the pain:** "Struggling with compliance? We can help." — struggling how? What does it look like when the problem is bad? Generic problem framing signals that the company doesn't actually know its customer.

**Universal collateral structural patterns:**

- CTA inflation: more than one primary CTA competing for attention in a section; readers ignore all of them
- Hero claim not supported in the body: if the headline makes a claim, the body needs to substantiate it with evidence
- All benefits, no mechanism: outcomes listed without any explanation of how the product delivers them
- No specificity anywhere: no named customers, no numbers, no named problems; if everything is generic, nothing is credible
- Benefit bullet overload: 6-8 bullets all at the same weight; readers retain nothing; cut to 3, ordered by what the ICP cares most about

### 50. Press Release AI Patterns

**Phrase-level:**
- "We are thrilled/excited/delighted/pleased to announce" — state the news in the first sentence without an emotion qualifier
- "This partnership/acquisition/launch represents a significant milestone" — significant for whom and how? Specify.
- Wire service filler: "announced today that", "is pleased to announce", "has entered into an agreement to" — say the thing directly
- "Going forward", "moving forward", "in the coming months" — vague temporal filler; use a specific date or omit

**Executive quote patterns:**
- Quote says nothing specific: any quote where a different executive at a different company could have said the same thing. Example: "We are excited about the opportunities this presents for our customers" — who, specifically? What opportunities? What customers?
- Quote repeats the headline in different words: the quote should add information the headline doesn't contain
- Quote is policy-speak: "We remain committed to delivering value across our portfolio" — this is not a human thought

**Structural:**
- Buried news: the actual announcement appears in paragraph 2 or 3 after context-setting. The first sentence should be the news.
- No actual news: if nothing changed for the customer, the press release shouldn't exist. "Company continues to grow" is not a press release.
- Boilerplate-heavy: more than 20% of the release is standard boilerplate text. Cut context that doesn't add information.

### 51. One-Pager AI Patterns

- More features than benefits: a one-pager should answer "why should I care?" not "what does it do?"
- No named ICP: if it's for everyone, it's for no one; by the end of the first paragraph the reader should feel specifically addressed
- No differentiation: what does this do that an alternative doesn't? If the answer isn't on the page, readers will default to the incumbent
- Stat bomb without context: "10x faster", "50% reduction" — faster than what? Reduction from what baseline? Over what time period? Unanchored stats are not evidence.
- Section headers as filler: "Overview", "Key Benefits", "How It Works", "Why [Company]" — these are placeholders, not communication; replace with headers that make a claim

### 52. Webpage and Sales Deck AI Patterns

**Webpage:**
- CTA inflation: more than two distinct CTAs competing for attention on the same page
- Hero-body mismatch: the hero makes a claim the body doesn't support or address
- Social proof logos with no context: a row of logos without a quote, metric, or outcome attached is decoration, not evidence
- Section headers as filler: "Why [Company]?", "The [Company] Difference" — these are placeholders; make them claims instead

**Sales deck:**
- Problem slide too abstract: the problem should make the target buyer feel seen and slightly uncomfortable; if it could describe any company in any industry, rewrite it
- Solution slide leads with features: lead with the outcome the buyer cares about, then explain how the product delivers it
- "Our team has X years of combined experience" — irrelevant credential; replace with what that experience produced
- Generic competitive positioning: "we're the only platform that..." claims require evidence; vague differentiation is worse than no differentiation because it sounds like a claim but proves nothing
